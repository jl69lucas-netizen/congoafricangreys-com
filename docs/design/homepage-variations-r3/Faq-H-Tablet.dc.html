What Do Buyers Ask Most Before Choosing One of Our African Greys?

21 questions across four topics. The panel height stays fixed however many there are.

  • Are your African Grey parrots CITES documented?Yes. African Greys are listed on Appendix I of the treaty, its strictest tier, applied in 2017 to stop commercial trade in wild-caught birds.
  • Are the birds DNA-sexed and vet-checked?Yes. Each Grey's sex is confirmed by a PCR DNA-sexing test, and an avian veterinarian examines the bird before placement, with PBFD and Polyomavirus screening as part of our health protocol.
  • Are African Grey parrots legal to own in the United States?Yes. The treaty listing bans commercial trade in wild-caught birds, but aviary-hatched African Greys are legal to own and transfer within the U.S. with proper documentation.
  • How can I verify an African Grey breeder before paying?A real breeder can show a verifiable federal animal-welfare licence, hands over the bird's own origin paperwork, will video-call to show your specific bird on demand, accepts reversible payment (card, PayPal Goods, bank wire), and has a findable physical location.
  • How much does an African Grey parrot cost?Our Congo African Greys range from $1,700 to $2,500 for individual birds, with the bonded Jins + Jeni pair at $3,500.
  • Do you ship African Grey parrots nationwide?Yes. We ship IATA-compliant to all 50 states under the IATA Live Animals Regulations (LAR).
  • Is the deposit refundable?The $200 deposit holds your bird and is applied to the final price.
  • Why are African Grey parrots so expensive?The price reflects ethical captive breeding: months of daily hand-feeding, lab sexing, an avian-vet exam, the treaty paperwork, and a closed band — plus the fact that wild trade is banned, so every lawful bird was hatched in an aviary.
  • What age should I buy an African Grey?Buy a fully weaned bird — never an unweaned chick.
  • What is the difference between a Congo and a Timneh African Grey?Both are African Greys. The Congo (Psittacus erithacus) is larger (400–600g) with a bright red tail and a famous talking vocabulary.
  • How long do African Grey parrots live?African Greys live 40 to 60 years in captivity, and well-cared-for birds frequently reach the upper end of that range.
  • Do African Grey parrots really talk?Yes — the African Grey is the parrot most often cited for speech.
  • Are African Greys loud?Compared with macaws and cockatoos, African Greys are relatively quiet.
  • How big do African Grey parrots get?A Congo African Grey reaches about 12–14 inches long and 400–600 grams.
  • Male or female African Grey — which is better?Behaviorally, male and female African Greys are very similar; personality is shaped far more by individual temperament and upbringing than by sex.
  • What do African Grey parrots eat?A healthy African Grey eats a formulated pellet diet as its base, supplemented with fresh vegetables, leafy greens, and limited fruit.
  • Do African Greys need a companion bird?No — a single African Grey bonds deeply with its human family and does not require another bird.
  • Is an African Grey a good choice for a first-time bird owner?It can be, with preparation.
  • Are African Grey parrots good pets?For the right owner, the African Grey is one of the most rewarding companion birds in the world — affectionate, endlessly intelligent, and capable of genuine conversation.
  • Can an African Grey be left alone during the day?A Grey can be alone for a normal work day if it has a large cage, foraging toys, and rotated enrichment — but it should not be isolated for long stretches every day.
  • Do African Greys bond with only one person?Congo African Greys in particular tend to choose a favorite person, but early, consistent handling by the whole household builds a bird that is comfortable with several people.
