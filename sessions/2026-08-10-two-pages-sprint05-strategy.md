# PAGE A — `/buy-african-grey-parrots-with-shipping/`

## A1 · The strategic problem, stated once

The page holds **789 impressions at position 15.28** while ranking for **zero** of the queries it targets —
shipping intent is 0/0/0 across all 792 Google query rows. It earns on borrowed relevance. Meanwhile:

- **6 of 8 PAA slots** across both buy queries are **price** questions.
- Google's **AI Overview** on `buy african grey parrot online` tells buyers *"prices under $2,000 are nearly always fraudulent"* and puts a typical Congo at **$8,000–$8,550**. Three of our six birds sit under $2,000.
- The unqualified head term **fires a local pack**; the `online` / `usa` qualified variants do not.

**So the page must do three things at once:** rank for a buy cluster nobody on our site owns, survive an AI
Overview that pre-frames our price as fraud, and convert.

## A2 · Three strategies

### Strategy A-1 — "The Price Court"
**Organizing principle:** the page is an argument about price. Open on the contradiction (Google says
$8,000; three platforms show one seller at $7,500 / $8,500 / $4,500 on the same day), then the evidence, then
the birds as the conclusion. Shipping and documentation are exhibits.

- **Structure:** Hook → the artifact proof → what an honest price is made of → the birds → documentation → shipping → guarantee → FAQ
- **Frameworks:** PAS × EEBP × QAB
- **Voice lever:** *artifact vs price* — "three numbers for one bird"
- **Wins:** owns the dominant micro-intent outright; unassailable by any competitor on the SERP; strongest AEO/GEO citation surface.
- **Loses:** a buyer who simply wants to see birds has to read an argument first. **Defensive framing risk is real** — a page that leads by defending its prices can read as a page with something to defend.

### Strategy A-2 — "The Qualified Storefront"
**Organizing principle:** build the structure that is *measured* to rank for these terms — the
`buyafricangreyparrots.com` skeleton (trust pillars → named birds → benefits → guarantee → shipping → FAQ) —
but deeper on every axis, and aimed at the `online` / `usa` qualified variants rather than the pack-bearing
head term.

- **Structure:** trust pillars → bird cards → why an African Grey → guarantee → shipping → Congo-vs-Timneh → FAQ
- **Frameworks:** EEBP × FAB × QAB
- **Wins:** the only structure with live proof of ranking for our exact target; fastest to convert; easiest to write.
- **Loses:** **handles price only in the FAQ**, which directly contradicts locked constraint 2 and wastes the strongest evidence we hold. Also concedes the qualified-variant ceiling — those variants have lower volume than the head term.

### Strategy A-3 — "Proof-of-Price Storefront" ⭐ **(Recommended)**
**Organizing principle:** the storefront skeleton that is proven to rank, with the price-artifact proof
promoted from FAQ material to a first-class section placed **directly beneath the bird cards** — so the buyer
meets the prices, then immediately meets the reason they are what they are.

- **Structure:** trust ribbon → bird cards with prices → **the price-artifact proof** → documentation → guarantee (72-hour, then enforceability) → shipping as proof → geo (IA/KY/MD/TN/WI) → Congo-vs-Timneh → scam counter-positioning → FAQ
- **Frameworks:** **EEBP × PAS × QAB × EEAT** — EEBP on every header per the for-sale skill; PAS carries the price section only
- **Voice lever:** *artifact vs price*
- **Primary target:** `buy african grey parrot` (head, ~168 impressions across its close variants) — competing organically beneath the pack — while **winning** `buy african grey parrot online`, `buy african grey parrots in usa`, `african grey parrot price in usa`

**WHY this one, from the data:**
1. **The skeleton is the only one measured to rank.** `buyafricangreyparrots.com` holds #2 and #7 on our two target queries with exactly this section order. A-1 invents a structure no ranking page uses.
2. **Placement solves A-1's defensive-framing problem without paying A-2's price.** Prices appear first as facts on cards; the argument arrives after, as explanation rather than defence. The order is the whole difference.
3. **It satisfies locked constraint 2 structurally** — the proof is a section, not an FAQ row.
4. **It is where the PAA is.** 6 of 8 slots are price; a page that answers price *structurally* is answering the actual query set.

**TRADE-OFF, named:** this is the **longest** of the three and the slowest to build — the price-artifact
section needs live, sourced, dated figures and will need re-verification before any future edit, because it
cites third-party prices that move. A-2 would ship faster and convert nearly as well for buyers who never
question price at all.

# PAGE B — `/african-grey-parrots-for-sale-near-me/`

## B1 · The strategic problem, stated once

**Seven of nine ranking organic URLs are geo-scoped**, and both national pages rank below every geo page. The
local pack is three bird shops with no breeder among them. A national page in Midland cannot enter that pack
and cannot out-rank a state URL on a state query.

**But we own 24 state URLs and 15 metro URLs** — the exact shape BirdsNow uses to hold #1 — and **no page
routes to them**. Meanwhile a **54-click** legacy 301 already lands here, on a 1-H2 stub.

And the searcher arriving is not who the plan assumed: **`cheap` recurs on all five autosuggest stems,
`craigslist` on three**, and Craigslist ranks #4. This is the population the AI Overview says is being
defrauded.

## B2 · Three strategies

### Strategy B-1 — "The Switchboard"
**Organizing principle:** pure routing. Grid at the top, minimal prose, shortest possible path from arrival to
a state page.

- **Wins:** fastest to build; maximal equity flow to 40 destinations; unambiguous job.
- **Loses:** **a bare switchboard has no reason to rank for anything itself** and nothing to say to the 54 clicks already arriving. It also abandons the `cheap`/`craigslist` searcher entirely — handing them straight back to Craigslist.

### Strategy B-2 — "The Local Answer"
**Organizing principle:** write it the way the regional pages that outrank us are written — inventory,
locality, service area — and treat the grid as secondary navigation.

- **Wins:** mirrors what demonstrably ranks on this SERP.
- **Loses:** **we are not local to the searcher.** A regional page wins because it is genuinely near someone; imitating its form without its substance is the exact failure `/where-to-buy-african-greys-near-me/` already demonstrates — position 8.74, **zero clicks on 109 impressions**.

### Strategy B-3 — "Router With a Reason" ⭐ **(Recommended)**
**Organizing principle:** the grid stays high because routing is the job and geo-URLs are what win — but it
is preceded by a short, honest opener that captures the variance and states the real radius, and followed by
the section the `cheap`/`craigslist` searcher actually needs.

- **Structure:** hero → honest-radius opener (*"near me" means one of two things*) → **the 40-destination grid, state tier then metro tier** → bird cards + prices → what arrives with every bird (docs) → shipping/pickup tiers → **the cheap-and-Craigslist section** (price-artifact proof, reused not duplicated; links the scam cluster) → guarantee → FAQ incl. 3-3-3 → form
- **Frameworks:** **EEBP × BAB × QAB** — BAB carries the radius opener (*before:* you searched "near me" and got classifieds; *after:* you know which of 39 pages is yours)
- **Voice lever:** *near vs reachable* — Midland pickup within 2–3 hours; everywhere else flies

**WHY this one, from the data:**
1. **The grid must be high** — 7 of 9 ranking URLs are geo-scoped; the routing function is the only thing on this page that matches what Google rewards here.
2. **B-1's fatal flaw is measured, not theoretical.** `/where-to-buy-african-greys-near-me/` is a thin near-me page at position 8.74 taking **0 clicks on 109 impressions**. Thinness is exactly why it is being retired; rebuilding thinness at a new URL repeats the error.
3. **The opener earns the variance.** The `gray` spelling out-earns `grey` **5 clicks to 0** at position 9.56 vs 18.89. Prose that naturally carries both spellings and both singular/plural forms is how one page holds a 55-query cluster.
4. **The scam section is where the searcher is.** `cheap` on 5/5 stems, `craigslist` on 3/5, Craigslist at #4.

**TRADE-OFF, named:** every word above the grid delays the routing click, and routing is the page's primary
job. The opener must stay **short** — if it grows past a couple of short paragraphs it becomes B-2 by
accident, and B-2 is the strategy we rejected. The scam section sits **below** the grid for the same reason.

## D · Open Flags

1. **Page A's price-artifact section cites live third-party prices** ($7,500 / $8,500 / $4,500, measured 2026-08-10). These move. The section must carry its measurement date in the prose, and **any future edit to that section requires re-verification** — a stale competitor price presented as current would be exactly the kind of fabricated claim rule 10 forbids. Recommend wording that is durable (*"on a single day in August 2026 we recorded…"*) rather than present-tense.
2. **The 3-3-3 rule is unverified.** It appears in PAA on 3 of 3 queries, but we have no ledger entry defining it. **Before it goes in either FAQ, the answer must be sourced** — it is an animal-behaviour claim, not a C.A.Gs credential. Cheapest capture available, but not assertable until sourced.
3. **Task 5 must draft H1 variants for both pages** (5 A/B variants for H1 and each major H2, breeder picks). Not started here by design — the outline gate is its own approval.
